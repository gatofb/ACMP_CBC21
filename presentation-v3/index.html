How can we move beyond instant answers to truly learn in the age of AI?

Academia ComunicaOctober 2026
Start with the question. Everything that follows is one answer to it, tested first with students entering university in Buenos Aires.

What does learning mean? · Make it simple

If you can explain it, you understand it.

One definition before anything else. Recognizing something isn't knowing it. Repeating it isn't either. Learning is being able to explain it in your own words.

An analogy

Learning is crossing a desert

The crossingSlow and tiring, and it's what changes you. Every step you take yourself stays with you.

The shortcutYou get there on time but you never crossed. You spend the hours and keep nothing.

Enjoy the journey, not just the destination. The path is where learning happens.

If someone flies you over the desert, you arrive, but you can't cross it again on your own. Now let's look at how many students in Argentina actually make the crossing.

At UBA, the real entrance exam is the first year: the CBC.

UBA lets everyone in. The filter comes later: the first year is where most students drop out. The national figures are for Argentina as a whole, not only UBA. These students aren't failing because they're not smart. They were never taught how to learn.

And now

AI makes the shortcut free

A study with about 1,000 high-school students: one group practiced with a general-purpose AI, then took the exam without it.

+48%

better on practice problems, with AI.

−17%

worse on the exam, without it, than students who never had it.

Bastani et al., PNAS, 2025 · Türkiye, not Argentina. A version that guided instead of answering removed the drop.

Performance went up, learning went down. AI did the part that creates learning. But notice the last line: a version built to guide instead of answer didn't hurt. The problem isn't AI. It's what we ask it to do.

Our answer

PrepTest: a tutor that makes you explain

1

Find what you don't know

A diagnostic in the real exam format. After each answer you say how sure you are. Sure and wrong is a blind spot.

2

Explain it

No options to pick from. You explain the idea in your own words, typed or spoken.

3

Get feedback

The tutor checks your explanation against the course text and shows exactly what's missing.

Then your map updates, and the next session starts with your blind spots.

Three steps. The key is step 2: there's nothing to pick, so you can't guess and you can't just recognize the answer. You have to produce it. The tutor uses the course's own texts, so it never invents.

The first lab

Starting where students drop out

The CBC at UBA, through UBA XXI. We started with Law.

We start exactly where the problem is worst: the first year. Live today: ICSE and Sociology; IPC is in progress. In five real ICSE exams there are 90 questions but only 64 different ideas: they come back reworded, so memorizing answers doesn't work and understanding the idea does. If they ask about the exam: 18 multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes, no partial credit.

Why AI · Connecting the dots

"You can't connect the dots looking forward." Steve Jobs, Stanford, 2005

Explaining shows what you knowLearning science

The same ideas come back, rewordedThe real exams

Sure and wrong is a blind spotConfidence

Open answers can now be read at scaleAI

AI is good at assessing what we don't know

Used to answer, AI skips the learning. Used to assess, it reads every explanation, checks it against the source and finds the exact gap. No teacher can do that for every student, before every exam.

This is why AI, and why now. Looking back, the dots connect. What was missing was someone to read thousands of open explanations. That's AI enablement: not teaching people to prompt, but to learn with AI.
