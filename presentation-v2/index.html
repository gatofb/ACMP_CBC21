How can we move beyond instant answers to truly learn in the age of AI?

Using AI to build the human capacity to learn, not to replace it. Our first lab: the entry year at the University of Buenos Aires.

Academia Comunica de Mai PistinerOctober 2026
This is my personal challenge, and it's bigger than a university entrance exam: keeping the learning process alive at a time when answers are instant. PrepTest for UBA XXI is where I'm testing it first.

What does learning mean? · Make it simple

If you can explain it, you understand it.

Before talking about the problem, one definition. Learning isn't recognizing or repeating: it's being able to explain it in your own words.

The problem

We're learning to get answers, not to think

Learning used to mean a journey. Today you can skip most of it and get an answer that looks perfect in seconds.

Today: a polished answer in seconds
1Meet a problem
2Try to solve it
3Get it wrong
4Check what you knew
5Ask for help
6Try again

The paradox: AI can help us do things we don't know how to do yet.

But doing them with AI doesn't mean we've learned to do them.

The problem is much bigger than getting into university: we're losing the learning process in a society that rewards instant answers. Every step in that row is where learning happens, and it's exactly what the shortcut skips.

Read the two Bastani numbers together: AI made practice look much better, and the exam got worse. Performance went up, learning went down. The guided version is the hint for what to build.

Problem statement

The problem, defined

The problem

Students struggle to build deep, independent learning, in a system that often rewards finishing tasks and passing tests over learning how to learn.

The new tension

AI gives correct answers instantly, and makes it easy to skip what learning needs: effort, reasoning, mistakes, reflection and practice.

The risk

People who can produce results with technology, but can't understand, question or solve problems on their own.

If we had to write it as a startup problem statement, this is it. The third line is the one to stop on.

An analogy

Learning is crossing a desert

The crossingSlow and tiring, and it's what changes you. Every step you take yourself stays with you.

The shortcutYou get there on time but you never crossed. You spend the hours and keep nothing.

Enjoy the journey, not just the destination. The path isn't how you get to the result: it's where learning happens.

If someone flies you over the desert, you arrive but you can't cross it again on your own. In education the path isn't just a way to the result. The path is the learning.

The difficulty that matters is the effort of retrieving, reasoning and getting it wrong. The friction that doesn't matter: not knowing what to study, or which video covers it. We keep the first and remove the second.

Memorizing answers doesn't work. Understanding the idea does.

UBA XXI is the first lab, not the end goal. High volume, high stakes, and an exam that punishes almost-knowing. We read the real exams before building anything.

Principles of the solution

Built on six principles

1

Training, not more content

UBA XXI already has videos and guides. We tell you which one to watch, down to the minute.

2

The unit is the idea

We teach claims ("According to Romero…"), not chapters. Questions, videos and your score all hang from them.

3

Confidence finds blind spots

After every answer: sure, unsure or guessed. Sure and wrong is a blind spot, and it goes first.

4

Explain, don't pick

Practice has no options. You explain, typed or spoken, and the tutor works out what you'd have marked.

5

Check against the source

The tutor grades against the author's text, not general knowledge. It never invents quotes or facts.

6

Readiness as a range

"Today you're between 5 and 6.5." Plus what would get you to a 7, the grade that exempts you from the final.

MapPreparePracticeFeedback→ the map updates, and the cycle starts again
Six decisions that make this different from a question bank. The most important are 3 and 4: we measure confidence to find blind spots, and in practice there are no options to pick from.

Connecting the dots

"You can't connect the dots looking forward." Steve Jobs, Stanford, 2005

Open answers can now be read at scaleAI

AI is good at assessing what we don't know

Used to answer, AI skips the learning. Used to assess, it reads every explanation, checks it against the source and finds the exact gap. No teacher can do that for every student, before every exam.

Looking back, the dots connect. Explaining is the best test, the exams repeat ideas, and confidence exposes blind spots. What was missing was someone to read thousands of open explanations. AI is bad as a shortcut and very good as an assessor. That's the role we give it.

Why this path · AI enablement

AI as a cognitive coach

Instead of working only as an answer engine, AI can watch, challenge and personalize each person's learning process.

1

Asks questions

Before it gives anything, it asks what you think.

2

Spots contradictions

It notices when two things you said don't fit.

3

Gives hints, step by step

Just enough to keep you moving, never the whole answer.

4

Asks you to explain

Your explanation is the exercise, and the evidence.

5

Checks you without help

Understanding counts when you can show it alone.

AI enablement isn't teaching people to prompt. It's teaching them to learn with AI, and that skill carries over to everything else.

This is why we went this way. AI enablement usually means "go faster with these tools". The deeper skill is using AI to understand faster without skipping the understanding.

Correct answers aren't enough. The map already tracks two of these: confidence vs. correctness is metacognition, and a blind spot that's still green a week later is retention. With a first group of students we'll also compare the estimated grade with the real one.
